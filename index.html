Move a slider or enter exact days. Revenue and COGS stay at Before values; cash freed compares with Before.

Interest savings assume every freed dollar pays down the bank line, capped at its Before balance. Negative cash freed requires extra borrowing and produces a negative saving. The case itself keeps some cash instead.

Skipping the supplier discount is an annualized financing cost, not a dollar expense or a saving from paying late. Paying beyond the due date can put supplier relationships at risk.

How the math works

DIO · Days inventory outstanding
Inventory ÷ annual cost of goods sold (COGS) × days in year
DSO · Days sales outstanding
Accounts receivable ÷ annual revenue × days in year
DPO · Days payable outstanding
Accounts payable ÷ annual COGS × days in year
Cash conversion cycle (CCC)
DIO + DSO − DPO

Cash freed = Before − After for inventory and receivables; After − Before for payables. Positive amounts release cash; negative amounts use cash.

These are typical year-round balances. All sales are on credit and purchases equal COGS.
